Android apps

Your own server, as a real app

Muny, Hearth and Arden Hub run on your own hardware, at home. Their Android apps open your server in an app of its own, with no browser around it, and add what a web page can't.

Muny Personal finance and subscriptions. Hearth The family's start page for the home network. Arden Hub Your local AI companion, from your phone.

What the apps add

  • Your server, nobody else's. The app asks for your server's address the first time and only ever talks to it. Nothing is hosted for you, and nothing about you is sent to me.
  • Fingerprint or screen lock when the app opens, and again after a few minutes away.
  • Notifications under the app's own name and icon, encrypted on your server so that only your phone can read them.
  • Works offline where the server's web app does: it opens with what you last saw.
  • Shortcuts when you press and hold the icon, and downloads that land in Downloads or open in your phone's viewer.

What you need

  1. The server running at home. Each app's page links to its install guide (Docker, about ten minutes).
  2. An https:// address for it that your phone can reach, such as a Tailscale name, or a home name like https://muny.home with your home's certificate installed on the phone.
  3. The app, then enter that address when it asks.

The apps are free, with no ads, no tracking and no accounts with me. See Privacy for exactly what goes where.
